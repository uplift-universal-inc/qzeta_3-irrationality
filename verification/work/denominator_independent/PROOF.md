# Independent check of the corrected all-size denominator bound

**Verdict:** the correction by a rank bound is valid. Together with a sharper
cluster-variance estimate it gives a strict, though small, positive arithmetic
margin. This note checks the denominator argument only; it does not independently
certify the analytic or nonvanishing inputs of the proposed irrationality proof.

Let

\[
D_K(x)=\prod_{a=1}^K(1-z^{-a}x),\qquad
\Delta_K(X)=\det_{0\le i,j<K} L_X(x^{i+j}/D_K),
\]

with

\[
L_X(1)=0,\quad L_X(x^j)=j^2/(z^j-1)\ (j\ge1),\quad
L_X((1-z^{-a}x)^{-1})=X-S_a,
\]

\[
S_a=\sum_{n=1}^a z^n(z^n+1)/(z^n-1)^3.
\]

All coefficient denominators of this determinant belong to the localization
generated by powers of z and the monic factors z^n-1, 1<=n<=K.

## 1. A local lemma for rank corrections

Let R be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer t, and suppose A and B are
matrices over R with rank(B)<=r over its fraction field. Then

\[
v_t\det(A+t^{-1}B)\ge-r.
\]

Indeed, det(A+uB) is a polynomial in u of degree at most r, and its coefficients
are integral. The degree statement follows by expanding the determinant in
columns: every term selecting more than r columns from B vanishes. The same
argument applies in the ramified extension containing a square root of t; the
valuation may then take half-integer values but is normalized by v_t(t)=1.

## 2. Cluster basis and the general local estimate

Fix a primitive d-th root of unity zeta. Let t=z-zeta. Put
Q(x)=prod_{a=1}^K(x-z^a), and partition the nodes by a modulo d. For the cluster
A_r of size m_r put Q_r=prod_{a in A_r}(x-z^a), and use the basis

\[
F_{r,j}(x)=Q(x)Q_r(x)^{-1}(x-\zeta^r)^j,\qquad 0\le j<m_r.
\]

The basis transformation has unit determinant at t=0: modulo t the Chinese
remainder theorem for the distinct factors (x-zeta^r)^{m_r} gives independence.
Thus changing to this basis does not change the determinant valuation.

The polynomial part of F_{r,j}F_{s,k}/D_K has degree at most K-2 and coefficients
regular at t=0. Applying L_X gives poles of order at most one. The pole part is
zero when r!=s, since then Q divides the numerator. When r=s, its partial
fraction coefficient at node z^a has valuation at least j+k-(m_r-1).
Multiplication by X-S_a loses at most another three. Hence the block entries
from this part have valuation at least j+k-m_r-2.

The weights

\[
w_{r,j}=\max(1/2,(m_r+2)/2-j)
\]

make all entries integral after multiplying row and column (r,j) by t^{w_{r,j}}.
The resulting bound is

\[
-v_t(\Delta_K)\le\sum_rF(m_r),\qquad
F(m)=\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor+2m+1.
\]

There are d clusters when d<=K. Their sizes are floor(K/d) and ceil(K/d), so

\[
\sum_rm_r^2=K^2/d+d\alpha(1-\alpha)\le K^2/d+d/4,
\qquad \alpha=\{K/d\}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\sum_rF(m_r)\le K^2/(4d)+2K+17d/16.
\]

This is the corrected estimate used for d<=K/2. It improves the earlier
K^2/(4d)+(9/4)K+d by retaining the exact balanced-cluster variance.

## 3. Correct treatment of d>K/2

Write h=K-d. There are h clusters of size two. All singleton node indices lie
between h+1 and d. The only singleton whose S_a can have a pole is a=d.

The earlier statement K-2<d in this range is false. Instead, the only potentially
singular polynomial moment is the moment of x^d, since K-2<2d. For polynomials
f,g of degree<K define

\[
T_d(f,g)=[x^d]\,(fg/D_K)_+,
\]

where the subscript denotes polynomial quotient at infinity. This is a bilinear
form with regular coefficients at t=0. It annihilates every polynomial f of
degree<=d: then deg(fg)<=K+d-1 and the quotient has degree<d. Therefore

\[
\operatorname{rank}T_d\le\max(0,K-d-1)=\max(0,h-1).
\]

This rank bound is exact over the local field, not merely a rank statement about
a residue matrix. It remains valid after the cluster basis change and arbitrary
row/column multiplication.

Separate the Gram matrix into the regular polynomial moments and all simple-pole
terms, plus

\[
\frac{d^2}{z^d-1}\,T_d.
\]

For the first matrix, give weights 2 and 1 to a size-two cluster, weight 3/2 to
the singleton a=d, and weight zero to all remaining singletons. Its scaled
entries are integral: the costs are 6 per double cluster and 3 for a=d.
The scaled T_d has integral entries because the weights are nonnegative.
Its coefficient d^2/(z^d-1) is a uniformizer inverse times a unit. The local
rank lemma now proves

\[
-v_t(\Delta_K)\le6h+3+\max(0,h-1)\le7h+3.
\]

The harmless +3 bound covers d=K as well. For h>=1 the slightly sharper bound
is 7h+2.

## 4. Global clearing and growth

Define

\[
E_d=\begin{cases}
\lceil K^2/(4d)+2K+17d/16\rceil,&d\le K/2,\\
7(K-d)+3,&K/2<d\le K.
\end{cases}
\]

The preceding local estimates apply at every primitive d-th root and give

\[
\left(\prod_{d=1}^K\Phi_d(z)^{E_d}\right)\Delta_K(X)
\in\mathbb Z[z,z^{-1}][X].
\]

There is no omitted rational constant denominator: the starting localization
uses integer Laurent polynomials and monic denominators, so Gauss's lemma removes
the cyclotomic factors over the integers once they have been removed over Q(z).

For an integer p>=2 let C_K(p)=prod_{d<=K}Phi_d(p)^{E_d}. Since Phi_d(p) is
coprime to p, this clears the coefficient denominator away from primes dividing
p. The separate p-adic content theorem, if correct, proves the remaining
denominators are absent and proves C_K(p)Delta_K/p^{K(K^2-1)/2} is integral.

The standard totient summations give

\[
\sum_{d\le K/2}E_d\varphi(d)
\le\frac{161}{64\pi^2}K^3+O(K^2\log K),
\]

\[
\sum_{K/2<d\le K}E_d\varphi(d)
=\frac7{2\pi^2}K^3+O(K^2\log K).
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\log C_K(p)\le
\frac{385}{64\pi^2}(\log p)K^3+O_p(K^2\log K).}
\]

Here log Phi_d(p)=phi(d) log p+O_p(1) uniformly in d. This follows directly from
the Moebius product formula, since the error is bounded by the convergent sum
sum_{j>=1}|log(1-p^{-j})|. Also sum E_d=O(K^2 log K).

Numerically the leading constant is 0.609510245348438..., while 11/18 is
0.611111111111111.... The strict difference is 0.001600865762673....

More explicitly, the p-specialization and content inputs combine prime by prime:
the Laurent-polynomial conclusion implies C_K(p)Delta_K has no denominator
outside primes dividing p. At such a prime ell, the content theorem gives
v_ell(Delta_K)>=v_ell(p)K(K^2-1)/2 coefficientwise, and C_K(p) is a unit.
Dividing by p^{K(K^2-1)/2} is therefore integral at ell. At every other prime
p is a unit and C_K(p) already clears the denominator. This proves integer
coefficients globally, without needing the stronger assertion that the content
gain is exact.

## 5. Independent conclusion

The previously omitted polynomial correction does not invalidate the denominator
strategy. Its exact finite-rank structure repairs that gap. The corrected bound
does fit the budget 1/2+1/9, with no extrapolation from numerical determinants.
The strength of the overall candidate theorem still depends on independently
validating its functional identification, p-adic content/nonvanishing argument,
and analytic estimate.
